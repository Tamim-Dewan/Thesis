\begin{abstract}
This document presents the formal mathematical formulation of the Proactive Golden-Hour Bridge Model (PGBM), a UAV-assisted emergency response system for post-earthquake scenarios. The system operates as an online decision-making framework where Responder UAVs are dynamically assigned to detected survivors to provide immediate assistance during the critical "Golden Hour" before ground rescue teams physically arrive. The model captures the coupling between survivor task states, UAV dynamics, battery constraints, time-dependent urgency decay, and 3D routing feasibility. The global system state is formally defined, and the event-triggered decision policy is mathematically characterized.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
In the aftermath of a major earthquake, Scout UAVs can rapidly detect trapped survivors using thermal and optical sensors. However, Ground Rescue Teams face significant delays due to collapsed infrastructure and blocked roads, creating a deadly gap between detection and physical rescue. The Proactive Golden-Hour Bridge Model (PGBM) introduces an intermediate UAV-assisted response layer where Responder UAVs are dispatched immediately to provide emergency supplies, two-way communication, and GPS relay before ground teams arrive.

\subsection{System Overview}
The PGBM consists of four primary actors:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Scout UAVs}: Detect survivors and transmit detection packets.
\item \textbf{Command Center}: Maintains global state and executes online allocation/routing decisions.
\item \textbf{Responder UAVs}: Execute bridge missions to deliver aid.
\item \textbf{Ground Rescue Team}: Performs final physical extraction (outside core model).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Core Assumptions}
\begin{itemize}
\item Obstacles are static during a single mission (Version 1).
\item Communication between all actors is reliable.
\item Each Responder UAV executes one tour at a time, serving up to $K$ demand items (possibly multiple survivors, e.g., $K=4$).
\item Survivor tasks are independent except via shared tour capacity $K$, inventory $I_j$, and tour energy $E(\pi_j)$; each tour is non-preemptive (once dispatched, the order is fixed until return to base).
\end{itemize}

\section{System Entities and State Definitions}

\subsection{Survivor Task Model}
Each survivor detection is modeled as a task $i \in \mathcal{S}(t)$, where $\mathcal{S}(t)$ is the set of all known survivor tasks at time $t$. Survivor $i$ is defined as:

\begin{equation}
S_i = (x_i, y_i, z_i, t_i^{\text{det}}, c_i, \sigma_i, \mathcal{R}_i, a_i, \text{assigned\_to}_i)
\end{equation}

with the following parameters:

\textbf{Parameter} & \textbf{Symbol} & \textbf{Domain} \\
\midrule

Detection Time & $t_i^{\text{det}}$ & $\mathbb{R}^+$ \\
Confidence Score & $c_i$ & $[0,1]$ \\
Severity & $\sigma_i$ & $[0,1]$ \\
Demand Set & $\mathcal{R}_i$ & $\subseteq\{\text{med},\text{water},\text{food}\}$ \\
Assignment Flag & $a_i$ & $\{0,1\}$ \\
Assigned UAV & $\text{assigned\_to}_i$ & $\mathcal{D}(t) \cup \{\emptyset\}$ \\
\bottomrule

$\mathcal{R}_i$ is the set of relief items required by $i$ (e.g., $\{\text{med}\}$, $\{\text{med},\text{water}\}$) provided by the Scout/Command Center; $|\mathcal{R}_i|\geq1$ and $|\mathcal{R}_i|>1$ denotes multiple demands at the same location.
Severity $\sigma_i$ denotes the static medical criticality at detection (independent of time), with $\sigma_i=1$ representing the most critical condition. Two possible scenarios for obtaining $\sigma_i$ are: (1) \textit{Scout AI Inference}: estimated automatically from Scout UAV thermal/optical imagery and rubble context via a classification model (e.g., motion/heat signature analysis), and (2) \textit{Human-in-the-Loop}: tagged manually by the Command Center operator reviewing the Scout video feed.

\subsection{Urgency Decay Function}
The urgency of survivor $i$ decays exponentially with waiting time according to the Golden Hour principle:

\begin{equation}
u_i(t) = \sigma_i \cdot e^{-\alpha (t - t_i^{\text{det}})}
\label{eq:urgency}
\end{equation}

where $\sigma_i \in[0,1]$ is the severity at detection so that $u_i(t_i^{\text{det}})=\sigma_i$, $\Delta t = t - t_i^{\text{det}} \geq 0$ is the waiting time, and $\alpha>0$ is the decay rate. The rate is defined via the half-life $T_{\text{half}}$ (e.g., $T_{\text{half}}=30$ min) as

\begin{equation}
\alpha = \frac{\ln 2}{T_{\text{half}}},
\end{equation}

so that $u_i(t_i^{\text{det}}+T_{\text{half}})=\sigma_i/2$. For $T_{\text{half}}=30$ min, $\alpha \approx 0.023$ min$^{-1}$ ($3.8\times10^{-4}$ s$^{-1}$); $T_{\text{half}}=60$ min gives $\alpha\approx0.0115$ min$^{-1}$. A survivor found 10 min ago ($\Delta t=10$) has $u\approx0.79\sigma_i$, while one found 90 min ago has $u\approx0.12\sigma_i$ at the same current time $t$, capturing higher salvageability of fresher detections.

\subsection{Confidence Score and Verification Policy}
Each detection carries a confidence score $c_i \in [0,1]$ representing the Scout UAV detector's estimated probability that the detection is a true survivor (e.g., thermal/optical AI output). A threshold $c_{\text{thresh}} \in [0,1]$ (e.g., $0.5$) separates high- and low-confidence regimes:
\begin{equation}
\text{eligible}(i) = \begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } c_i \geq c_{\text{thresh}} \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Detections with $c_i \geq c_{\text{thresh}}$ enter the high-confidence queue $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{high}}(t)$ and are eligible for Responder assignment. Detections with $c_i < c_{\text{thresh}}$ enter the low-confidence queue $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{low}}(t)$ and are \textit{not} eligible for Responder dispatch; they require verification (Scout re-scan or operator review) before promotion to $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{high}}$. In Version 1, $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{low}}$ is modeled as a holding set and verification is abstracted as an exogenous promotion event. The offloading ILP is defined only over $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{high}}(t)$.

\subsection{Responder UAV Model}
Each Responder UAV $j \in \mathcal{D}(t)$ is defined as:

\begin{equation}
D_j = (x_j, y_j, z_j, p_j^{\text{base}}, B_j, K, I_j, m, A_j, s_j, \text{status}_j)
\end{equation}

with the following parameters:

$K$ is the maximum number of items, $I_j$ is the loaded inventory (e.g., $[ \text{med},\text{water},\text{food},\text{med}]$), and $m$ is the derived total weight of $I_j$ which decreases after each drop and affects $e_{\text{travel}}$ per leg.

\subsection{Availability States}
The availability $A_j$ is derived from the UAV's status:

\begin{equation}
A_j = \begin{cases}
1 & \text{if status}_j = \text{Idle} \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Environment Model}
The disaster environment is represented as continuous sets:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E} = (V, O)
\end{equation}

where $V \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ is navigable free space and $O \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ is the static obstacle set (buildings, rubble, no-fly zones), with $V \cap O = \emptyset$. For Version 1, $O(t)=O$ (static) and the hazard map $R: V \rightarrow [0,1]$ is set to $R=0$ and deferred. A route $P$ is feasible iff $P \subset V$ (all waypoints lie in free space).

\subsection{Task Queue}
The ordered queue of unassigned survivors is defined as a derived view:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{Q}(t) = \text{order}(\{ S_i \in \mathcal{S}(t) \mid a_i = 0 \})
\end{equation}

ordered by priority:
\begin{equation}
\text{priority}(i) = \beta_1 \cdot u_i(t) + \beta_2 \cdot c_i
\end{equation}
Note: $\mathcal{Q}(t)$ is not an independent state component but a derived view of $\mathcal{S}(t)$ for scheduling. With verification threshold $c_{\text{thresh}}$, the queue partitions as:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{Q}_{\text{high}}(t) &= \{ S_i \in \mathcal{Q}(t) \mid c_i \geq c_{\text{thresh}} \}, \\
\mathcal{Q}_{\text{low}}(t) &= \{ S_i \in \mathcal{Q}(t) \mid c_i < c_{\text{thresh}} \}
\end{align}
Only $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{high}}(t)$ is eligible for the offloading assignment; $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{low}}(t)$ is held for verification. \textit{Uncertainty handling:} The partition manages detection uncertainty (false positives from Scout thermal/optical AI). High-confidence detections are directly eligible, while low-confidence detections are buffered in $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{low}}$ for verification (re-scan/operator review) before promotion, preventing scarce Responder UAVs from being wasted on likely false alarms. Note: $\text{priority}(i)$ is a pre-filter ordering heuristic for $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{high}}(t)$ (e.g., for greedy fallback or tie-breaking); the optimal assignment is determined by the ILP cost $C_{ij}(t)$, not by priority order.

\section{Decision Variables}

\subsection{Tour Variable (Primary Decision)}
At any decision time $t$, the offloading decision for each available UAV $j$ is an ordered tour

\begin{equation}
\pi_j(t) = (i_1, i_2, \dots, i_k), \quad k = \text{len}(\pi_j), \quad \sum_{i\in\pi_j} |\mathcal{R}_i| \leq K
\end{equation}

where $i_l \in \mathcal{Q}_{\text{high}}(t)$ is the $l$-th survivor to be served, $|\mathcal{R}_i|$ is the number of items demanded by $i$, and $K$ (e.g., 4) is the UAV item capacity. Thus $\pi_j$ encodes both \textit{selection} (which survivors) and \textit{order} (in which sequence). For example, $\pi_j=(A,C)$ with $\mathcal{R}_A=\{\text{med}\}$ and $\mathcal{R}_C=\{\text{water},\text{food}\}$ carries $|\pi_j|=3\leq4$ items to two survivors; $\pi_j=(C,A)$ is a different tour with different travel distance, climb, and payload-dependent energy. An empty tour $\pi_j=\emptyset$ means the UAV stays idle at base. The set of all tours $\{\pi_j\}_{j\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{avail}}}$ partitions $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{high}}$ (each survivor in at most one tour).

\subsection{Tour Route (Geometric Realization)}
A tour $\pi_j$ is realized geometrically as the concatenation of its $k+1$ optimal leg paths:

\begin{equation}
P_{\pi_j} = P_{p_j \to p_{i_1}}^* \circ P_{p_{i_1} \to p_{i_2}}^* \circ \cdots \circ P_{p_{i_k} \to p_j^{\text{base}}}^*
\label{eq:tour}
\end{equation}

where each leg $P_{a\to b}^*=\{a=p_0,p_1,\dots,p_L=b\}$ is the cheapest feasible waypoint sequence with $p_k\in V$. If any leg has no feasible path in $V$, the whole tour is infeasible. The routing oracle provides each leg: $\text{RoutingOracle}(p_a,p_b)\to(T_{ab},E_{ab})$ or $(\infty,\infty)$ if infeasible.

\section{Cost Functions}

\subsection{Path Cost and Optimal Path}
For any path $P=\{p_0,\dots,p_L\}\subset V$, the traversal cost is defined as its energy:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Cost}(P)=E(P)&=\sum_{k=1}^{L} e_{\text{travel}}(p_k,m,w_0)\|p_k-p_{k-1}\| \\
&\quad + e_{\text{vert}}(m)\max(0,z_k-z_{k-1})
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:pathcost}
\end{equation}

where $L$ is the number of waypoints and $e_{\text{travel}},e_{\text{vert}}$ are defined below. The cheapest feasible path between two points is

\begin{equation}
P_{a\to b}^*=\arg\min_{P\subset V,\,p_0=a,\,p_L=b}\text{Cost}(P)
\label{eq:optpath}
\end{equation}

If no $P\subset V$ exists, the path is infeasible. The routing oracle encapsulates this: $\text{RoutingOracle}(a,b)$ returns $(T_{ab},E_{ab})$ for $P_{a\to b}^*$ or $(\infty,\infty)$ if infeasible.

\subsection{Tour Time and Energy}
For a tour $\pi_j=(i_1,\dots,i_k)$, let $P_0=P_{p_j\to p_{i_1}}^*$, $P_l=P_{p_{i_l}\to p_{i_{l+1}}}^*$ ($1\le l<k$), and $P_k=P_{p_{i_k}\to p_j^{\text{base}}}^*$. With $v_{\max}$ the maximum speed and $T_{\text{hover}}=T_{\text{drop}}+T_{\text{verify}}=15$ s per stop,

\begin{equation}
T(\pi_j)=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{\|P_l\|}{v_{\max}}+\sum_{i\in\pi_j}T_{\text{hover}}(i)
\end{equation}

Let $m_0=\sum_{item\in I_j}weight(item)$ and $m_l=m_{l-1}-\sum_{item\in\mathcal{R}_{i_l}}weight(item)$ be the payload before leg $l$. Then

\begin{equation}
E_{\text{leg}}(l)=e_{\text{travel}}(p_l,m_{l-1},w_0)\|P_l\|+e_{\text{vert}}(m_{l-1})\Delta z_l
\end{equation}

with $e_{\text{travel}}(p,m,w_0)=e_0+k_m m+k_w w_0$, $e_{\text{vert}}(m)=mg/\eta_{\text{vert}}$ ($m,w_0$ realtime; $w_0$ uniform wind, $w(p)$ deferred; $e_0,k_m,k_w,\eta_{\text{vert}}$ calibrated offline). The total tour cost and energy are

\begin{equation}
E(\pi_j)=\sum_{l=0}^{k}E_{\text{leg}}(l)+\sum_{i\in\pi_j}e_{\text{hover}}T_{\text{hover}}(i)
\end{equation}

Early legs carry larger $m$ and thus higher $e_{\text{travel}}$. The single-leg case $k=1$ gives $E_{ij}^{\text{total}}=E(\pi_j=(i))$.

\subsection{Coupling to Offloading}
Routing provides $(T(\pi_j),E(\pi_j))$ via the oracle for each candidate tour. Offloading uses them in the feasibility check \eqref{eq:battery} and tour cost \eqref{eq:cost}. All $k+1$ legs must be feasible; otherwise $C(\pi_j)=\infty$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:coupling}).

\subsection{Tour Assignment Cost}

\begin{equation}
C(\pi_j,t) = \begin{cases}
\sum_{i\in\pi_j}\left[w_T \frac{T_{\text{leg}}(i)}{T_{\max}} + w_E \frac{E_{\text{leg}}(i)}{E_{\max}} - w_U u_i(t) c_i\right] & \text{if Feasible}(\pi_j) \\
\infty & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}

where $w_T, w_E, w_U > 0$ are tour cost weights ($w_T+w_E+w_U=1$ baseline), $T_{\max}=\max_{\text{feasible}}T_{\text{leg}}$ (e.g., $600$ s for $2$km map at $v_{\max}=10$ m/s) and $E_{\max}=B_{\max}$ (e.g., $50000$ J) are normalization constants (alternatively adaptive $T_{\max}(t)=\max_{i\in\mathcal{Q}_{\text{high}}}T_{ij}$ per snapshot), and $-w_U u_i c_i$ rewards urgency/confidence per survivor in the tour. Infeasible tours have $\infty$ cost. The single-pair $C_{ij}(t)$ is the special case $k=1$ where $\pi_j=(i)$, i.e., $C_{ij}=C(\pi_j=(i))$.

\textit{Rationale:} The tour cost is a dimensionless trade-off among time, energy, and expected value summed over the tour; $C_{ij}$ is $k=1$.

\section{Feasibility Constraints}

\subsection{Battery Constraint for Tour}
A tour $\pi_j$ is feasible only if the whole tour plus reserve fits the remaining battery:

\begin{equation}
E(\pi_j) + E_{\text{reserve}} \leq B_j(t)
\label{eq:battery}
\end{equation}

where $E(\pi_j)=\sum_{l}E_{\text{leg}}(l)+\sum_{i\in\pi_j}E_{\text{hover}}(i)$ from Step 4 and $E_{\text{reserve}}=0.2\,B_{\max}$.

\subsection{Demand Matching}
Each demand set must be satisfied from the remaining inventory:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}_{i_l} \subseteq I_j^{(l-1)} \quad \forall i_l\in\pi_j
\end{equation}

where $I_j^{(l-1)}$ is the inventory before stop $l$ ($I_j^{(0)}=I_j$). If $\mathcal{R}_{i_l}\not\subseteq I_j^{(l-1)}$, the tour is infeasible.

\subsection{Reachability per Leg}
Each leg must lie in free space: $P_{l}\subset V$ for all $l$, i.e., $p_k\in V$ for every waypoint. Reachability is an oracle per leg; one infeasible leg makes the whole tour infeasible.

\subsection{Capacity and Availability}
$\sum_{i\in\pi_j}|\mathcal{R}_i|\leq K$ (total items) and $A_j=1$ (Idle). If $A_j=0$ or capacity exceeded, the tour is infeasible. Here $\text{len}(\pi_j)=k$ is the number of survivors and $\sum|\mathcal{R}_i|$ is the number of items.

\subsection{Feasible Tour Condition}
A tour $\pi_j$ is feasible iff all of the above hold:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Feasible}(\pi_j) = &(A_j=1) \land (\sum_{i\in\pi_j}|\mathcal{R}_i|\leq K) \land (\forall i\in\pi_j: c_i\geq c_{\text{thresh}}) \\
&\land (\forall l: P_{l}\subset V) \land (\forall i_l\in\pi_j: \mathcal{R}_{i_l}\subseteq I_j^{(l-1)}) \\
&\land (E(\pi_j)+E_{\text{reserve}}\leq B_j(t))
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:feasible}
\end{equation}

\section{Assignment Constraints}

\subsection{Single Assignment per Survivor}
Each survivor in $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{high}}(t)$ appears in at most one tour:

\begin{equation}
\sum_{j} \mathbf{1}\{i\in\pi_j(t)\} \leq 1 \quad \forall i \in \mathcal{Q}_{\text{high}}(t)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Capacity per Tour}
Each tour respects item capacity:

\begin{equation}
\sum_{i\in\pi_j(t)} |\mathcal{R}_i| \leq K \quad \forall j \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{avail}}(t)
\end{equation}

where $K$ is the item capacity (e.g., 4), $\text{len}(\pi_j)=k$ is the number of survivors and $\sum|\mathcal{R}_i|$ is the number of items. A UAV may stay idle ($\pi_j=\emptyset$) if no feasible tour exists.

\subsection{Feasibility Enforcement}
If a tour $\pi_j$ is not feasible, its cost is set to infinity: $C(\pi_j)=\infty$ when $\neg\text{Feasible}(\pi_j)$ (includes $c_i<c_{\text{thresh}}$, $A_j=0$, $P_l\not\subset V$, $\mathcal{R}_{i_l}\not\subseteq I_j^{(l-1)}$, or $E(\pi_j)+E_{\text{reserve}}>B_j$). Any optimal solution will have such tours excluded.

\section{Objective Function}

\subsection{Primary Objective: Minimize Cost}
The snapshot problem $\text{OFF-SNAP}(t)$ over tours $\{\pi_j\}$ is:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\{\pi_j\}} \sum_{j\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{avail}}(t)} C(\pi_j)
\label{eq:obj}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
C(\pi_j)=\sum_{i\in\pi_j}\left[w_T\frac{T_{\text{leg}}(i)}{T_{\max}}+w_E\frac{E_{\text{leg}}(i)}{E_{\max}}-w_U u_i(t)c_i\right]
\label{eq:costpi}
\end{equation}
Note that the single-assignment cost $C_{ij}(t)$ in \S5.6 is the $k=1$ special case $C(\pi_j=(i))=C_{ij}(t)$.
where $T_{\text{leg}}(i),E_{\text{leg}}(i)$ include travel to $i$ plus $15$s hover. If $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{high}}=\emptyset$ or all $C(\pi_j)=\infty$, the optimum is $\pi_j=\emptyset$ (idle).

\subsection{State Update for Tours}
Dispatching $\pi_j=(i_1,\dots,i_k)$ updates: $\mathcal{Q}(t^+)=\mathcal{Q}(t^-)\setminus\{i_1,\dots,i_k\}$, $B_j(t^+)=B_j(t^-)-E(\pi_j)$, $I_j(t^+)=I_j(t^-)\setminus\bigcup_{i\in\pi_j}\mathcal{R}_i$, $m(t^+)=m(t^-)-\sum_{i\in\pi_j} weight(\mathcal{R}_i)$, $\text{status}_j\leftarrow\text{EnRoute}$ until return to $p_j^{\text{base}}$ for reload.

\section{State Transition Functions}

\subsection{New Detection}
\begin{align}
\mathcal{S}(t^+) &= \mathcal{S}(t^-) \cup \{S_{\text{new}}\} \\
\mathcal{Q}(t^+) &= \mathcal{Q}(t^-) \cup \{S_{\text{new}}\}
\end{align}

\subsection{Tour Dispatch}
Dispatching tour $\pi_j=(i_1,\dots,i_k)$:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{Q}(t^+) &= \mathcal{Q}(t^-) \setminus \{i_1,\dots,i_k\} \\
B_j(t^+) &= B_j(t^-) - E(\pi_j) \\
I_j(t^+) &= I_j(t^-) \setminus \bigcup_{i\in\pi_j}\mathcal{R}_i \\
m(t^+) &= \sum_{item\in I_j(t^+)} weight(item) \\
\text{status}_j &\leftarrow \text{EnRoute}
\end{align}

\subsection{Tour Completes (Return to Base)}
Upon return to $p_j^{\text{base}}$:
\begin{align}
\text{status}_j &\leftarrow \text{Idle} \\
p_j &\leftarrow p_j^{\text{base}} \\
I_j &\leftarrow \text{reload}(K) \\
m &\leftarrow \sum_{item\in I_j} weight(item) \\
A_j &\leftarrow 1
\end{align}

\section{Scope and Limitations}

\subsection{Core Model Includes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Survivor detection as exogenous input
\item Responder UAV assignment and routing
\item Battery-aware feasibility
\item Time-dependent urgency
\item Online, event-triggered decision making
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Excluded (Future Work)}
\begin{itemize}
\item Dynamic aftershock obstacles
\item UAV-to-UAV collision avoidance
\item Communication relay UAVs
\item Hot-swap battery stations
\item Detection uncertainty verification policies
\item Recourse/reassignment after dispatch
\item High-fidelity nonlinear energy/power model (mass, aerodynamic drag, wind, acceleration-aware)
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
This document presents a complete mathematical formulation of the Proactive Golden-Hour Bridge Model (PGBM) for post-earthquake UAV-assisted response. The model captures the essential dynamics of the system: survivor states with time-dependent urgency, Responder UAV states with battery constraints, and the online nature of detection arrivals and resource availability. The formulation separates feasibility (battery and routing constraints) from optimization (assignment and path selection), providing a clear foundation for algorithm design and simulation. The coupling between assignment and routing is explicitly defined through the tour cost function $C(\pi_j)$ (with $C_{ij}=C(\pi_j=(i))$ as the $k=1$ special case), which depends on travel time and energy per leg and urgency per survivor.
